# Writing a record well

## Statement

One sentence a subject expert could call right or wrong.

- Keep the speaker's own terms, and the detail that pins the point down: the number, the deadline, which customers.
- A need with a person behind it reads "As a <who> I want <what> so that <why>". A rule or a limit reads as a plain fact: "A refund for a card payment goes back to the card that paid."
- Two needs are two Requirements when each still stands with the other gone.
- `Source` is who said it and where: "Tom Ashby, workshop 9 March". For pasted notes with no names, the person you are talking to and "notes" with today's date.

## Example

One concrete case with names and numbers, and what happens: "A learner withdraws three days before the first session and gets nothing back."

A `proposed` example sits on an edge the material leaves open: exactly on the limit, just past it, two things at once. One or two for each rule.

## Question

The heading is the question itself, answerable in a sentence by one person. `Raised by` is whoever asked it; when the gap is one you noticed, the person you are talking to.

## Flow

Write a Flow when the material says who does what, and then what. When the person is there to ask and says "it depends", or a job passes between people, read [cards/walk-a-process.md](cards/walk-a-process.md).

- One step for each action by one person or team, named as that action: "Office cancels the class".
- Where the material says "if" or "unless", the step before is a decision: give it one `Go to` line for each branch, each with its `(when ...)`.
